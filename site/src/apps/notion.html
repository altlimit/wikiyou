Overview

Notion is a productivity and note-taking web application developed by Notion Labs, Inc., a company founded in 2013 and based in San Francisco, California. The application is organized around the idea of a single connected workspace, bringing together notes, documents, databases, wikis, and project and task management in one place rather than distributing them across separate tools.

Content in Notion is assembled from modular units commonly referred to as blocks, which users arrange into pages. Pages can be nested within one another, allowing a workspace to be structured hierarchically as it grows. The application is used by both individuals and teams, and is frequently described as an all-in-one workspace because of the range of document types it can accommodate.

Features

Notion's functionality is built on a small number of core concepts that are combined in different ways:

  • Blocks — Individual pieces of content, such as paragraphs of text, headings, lists, images, and embedded items, are handled as discrete blocks. Users add, rearrange, and nest these blocks to compose a page, which allows a single page to serve as anything from a short note to a structured document.
  • Databases — Collections of pages can be organized as databases, where each entry is itself a page with its own properties. Databases can be presented in different views, making them suitable for tracking tasks, projects, and other structured records alongside ordinary written content.
  • Wikis — Interlinked pages can be arranged into wikis and knowledge bases, giving teams a shared reference area for documentation. Because wiki pages use the same blocks and databases as the rest of the workspace, reference material and working documents can live side by side.
  • Project and task management — Tasks and projects can be maintained within the same workspace as the documents that describe them, so planning material and supporting notes remain connected.

Platforms

Notion is available in the web browser, which serves as its primary interface, and also as desktop and mobile applications. The desktop and mobile clients provide access to the same workspace as the browser version, allowing users to work across devices. Further product information is published on the company's official website.

Uses

Notion is commonly used for documentation, internal knowledge bases, and planning. Individual users apply it to personal note-taking, writing, and tracking their own projects, while teams use it as a shared workspace for reference material and coordinated work.

Because the application is assembled from general-purpose components rather than fixed, single-purpose screens, the same underlying features are adapted to a wide variety of workflows. A team may use databases for project tracking and wiki pages for onboarding documentation within one workspace, while an individual may use the same building blocks for a personal journal or reading list. This flexibility is a defining characteristic of the product, and it means that two Notion workspaces may look substantially different from one another despite being built from the same set of blocks.
