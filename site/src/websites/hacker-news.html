Overview

Hacker News is a social news website that focuses on computer science, technology, and entrepreneurship. It is run by the startup accelerator Y Combinator and was launched in 2007, having been created by Paul Graham. The site operates as a community-driven link aggregator: readers submit items, the community votes on and discusses them, and a ranking system determines which submissions are surfaced most prominently.

Hacker News is widely recognized for its deliberately minimal interface, which presents submissions as a plain ranked list of links with little visual styling, and for an audience drawn largely from programmers, startup founders, and other technologists. Because the site is operated by a startup accelerator, its subject matter tends to reflect the overlapping interests of software development and company building.

How It Works

The site is built around three core mechanics that together determine what appears on its front page:

  • Submissions — Registered users post content to the site, either as links to external pages or as text posts written directly on Hacker News. Submissions typically concern computer science, technology, or entrepreneurship, the three areas the site is oriented around.
  • Voting — Community members vote on submissions. These votes feed a ranking that surfaces popular items, so the ordering of the site reflects collective community judgment rather than editorial selection by a staff.
  • Comments — Each submission has an attached discussion where users comment on the item and respond to one another. Comments are themselves subject to community voting and discussion, making the conversation around a link as central to the site as the link itself.

Because ranking is driven by votes, items that attract early community interest can move toward the top of the listing, while less popular submissions remain further down. The result is a continuously changing front page assembled from the submissions and voting activity of the site's users.

Community

The Hacker News community consists largely of programmers, founders, and technologists, and the site's discussions reflect that composition. Threads frequently combine technical detail with commentary on business and startup matters, mirroring the dual focus on engineering and entrepreneurship that the site was organized around.

The minimal interface is itself a part of the site's identity. Rather than emphasizing images, video, or elaborate layout, Hacker News presents text and links with minimal ornamentation, placing the emphasis on the submitted material and the discussion attached to it. This design has remained a defining characteristic of the site since its launch.

Hacker News is accessible at its main site, where the current ranked list of submissions and their associated discussions can be read without an account. Submitting links and text posts, voting, and commenting are functions available to registered users.
